# SAHAYAK — Personnel Stress & Welfare Support

## Problem statement
Develop a privacy-preserving personnel welfare monitoring and decision-support system for CAPFs, Armed Forces and other uniformed services. Analyze authorized duty, deployment, leave, transfer, training and workload data alongside optional self-reported wellness information to identify potential early indicators of strain, emotional fatigue and welfare concerns. Provide understandable assessments to authorized welfare professionals and aggregated operational insights to commanders, enabling confidential counselling, welfare intervention, workload review and accountable follow-up.

The system must support people and organizational recovery practices. It must not diagnose psychiatric conditions, determine fitness for duty, recommend disciplinary action or silently monitor private communications.

## Context and gap
The supplied brief identifies long deployments, hazardous conditions, irregular duty, separation from family and stressful incidents. Organizational records and voluntary concerns are often disconnected. Manual observation and self-reporting may delay recognition and support. Personnel also need a credible privacy boundary before they will trust a wellness system.

## Target users
- Personnel: voluntary check-ins, personal trends, consent and support requests.
- Welfare professionals: contextual review, confidential conversation, interventions and follow-up.
- Commanders: aggregated workload patterns and scenario comparison, excluding individual wellness information.
- Future administrators: verified role provisioning, integration policies and security operations.

## Success criteria
1. A personnel check-in changes their transparent prototype index and is retained after reload.
2. A support request is visible in the welfare view without requiring a high score.
3. An officer can inspect factors, record a review and schedule a follow-up.
4. Commander responses contain only aggregated unit data.
5. Consent withdrawal removes stored voluntary values and check-ins from the demo.
6. Workload scenario comparison recalculates stated rules without pretending to predict health outcomes.
7. Record changes and explicit case-profile access events appear in the audit view.

## Current implementation
Three role views, 36 synthetic personnel across three units, persisted check-ins and cases, transparent rule explanations, filtering/search, support requests, follow-ups, per-person trends, aggregation, CSV summary export, scenario planning, consent controls, action history and this problem definition in the application.

## Honest AI boundary
This version implements the explainable baseline and workflow. It does not contain a trained ML model or call an LLM. Rule thresholds and priority categories are illustrative and unvalidated. A model trained on labels generated by these same rules would primarily learn to copy them; its accuracy would not demonstrate real-world stress detection. A research extension needs representative, authorized data, independent outcome labels, qualified domain review, calibration and appropriate subgroup evaluation before any operational use.

Scores are not clinical probabilities. Missing voluntary data adds no risk points, but reduces available evidence. The score cannot rule out distress. An urgent help request should ultimately use a separately approved human response protocol, never an arbitrary critical-score threshold.

## Scenario-planning boundary
The scenario planner subtracts selected night shifts and weekly hours from fictional duty records, then recalculates the organizational rule component. It does not predict reduced mental-health cases, guarantee recovery or account for staffing coverage. No real schedules are changed.

## Privacy model and limitations
The deployed design expects platform-authenticated private access and isolates each visitor's fictional workspace. The role selector changes demonstration perspectives; it is not production role authentication. Server responses are projected by the selected perspective, but the owner can deliberately switch views. The local development build permits a synthetic demo identity only in development mode. Production APIs reject requests lacking platform identity.

The prototype's database stores a JSON workspace per owner with optimistic concurrency protection. Audit events are operational demo history, capped at 500, not an immutable compliance ledger. No real medical information should be entered. Production needs true server-assigned membership and role policies, separated case storage, least privilege, encryption/key management, retention enforcement, incident response and independent security assessment.

## Future integrations
Authorized HR connectors, transfer/training/incident fields, validated periodic assessments, optional approved wearable inputs, reviewed translations, notifications, identity provisioning and offline conflict handling remain outside this initial implementation. None should be represented as live.

## Evaluation
Track review delay, requests addressed, follow-up completion, officer feedback on inappropriate alerts, missing-data behavior, user comprehension, privacy-policy adherence and accessibility. Any clinical effectiveness claim requires separate qualified evaluation. Changes after an intervention do not by themselves prove causation.
